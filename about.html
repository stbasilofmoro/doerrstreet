About the firm

Founded 1960s
Weddington, N.C.

A family built on the railroad

The trade came over
in a suitcase.

The Polivka family left the Carpathian Rusyn region and settled in Pittsburgh, bringing the only two things that travel well — a willingness to work and a refusal to do it badly. Basil Sr. put both into the railroad, and three generations have kept them there.

The inspectors who walk your track came off the Class I side of the industry. They hold current FRA Part 213 certification, and most of them spent their careers as railroad inspectors before they arrived here — which means they know what a track department expects, because they used to be the ones expecting it.

Sixty years

Roots in Pittsburgh,
track in the Southeast.

  1. 1940s Pittsburgh

    The Polivka family settles in Pittsburgh, carrying Old World craftsmanship into American industry.

  2. 1960s Ohio

    Basil Sr. founds a railroad contracting business, performing track maintenance for regional carriers.

  3. 1980s Polivka International

    The firm grows into a national contractor, eventually holding work with all seven Class I railroads.

  4. 2000s Southeast

    Focus narrows to inspection and maintenance for industrial facilities and short-line operators.

  5. Today Doerr Street Rail Co

    Sixty years of institutional knowledge, working out of Weddington, North Carolina.

What we hold to

Four things,
none of them negotiable.

  1. One

    Safety first

    Track safety is not graded on a curve. Your plant lead is held to the standard we operated under on Class I track.

  2. Two

    Integrity

    Every defect goes in the report, including the ones that are awkward and the ones nobody asked about.

  3. Three

    Experience

    Inspectors average fifteen years and more of Class I background. Nobody learns on your track.

  4. Four

    Commitment

    The job is not finished at the report. It is finished when the remediation is done and re-inspected.

Credentials

FRA Part 213,
Classes I through VI.

Every inspector maintains current FRA Part 213 certification across track Classes I to VI, with extensive Class I and industrial track experience behind it. Reports are third party, dated, and signed by the inspector who walked the track.
